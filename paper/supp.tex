\section{S1. Relation to the superseded version}
An earlier manuscript (``Nested Federated Learning'' / ``TierFed'' v2) reported results produced by code with the following defects, found in an internal audit and corrected here: (1) differential privacy was applied inside two strategies only and clipped the aggregate rather than each client's update, so FedAvg/FedPer/FedProx ran without noise and the reported budgets were invalid; the TierFed DP runs crashed on local-only rounds and the table was unsupported; (2) the SCAFFOLD baseline was FedAvg with doubled bit accounting; (3) the scalability table contained no TierFed runs and its values were not those of the logs; (4) the architecture sweep compared different depths across methods; (5) the imaging result used an undisclosed configuration on a binarised 97\%-positive task; (6) the inversion attack targeted the fixed schedule at initialisation and was labelled TierFed; (7) the model described (93 inputs) was not the model trained (162 inputs); (8) repeated runs were not deterministic; (9) no patient-level deduplication was performed and one ``missing specialty'' client held 49\% of the data; (10) the greedy controller reached only powers of two; (11) the fixed multi-rate schedule sent a fallback tier whenever no tier was due, which made its medium-tier period ineffective; (12) compressors were applied to raw weights instead of updates; (13) in v3, the DP comparison ran data-dependent schedulers (adaptive TierFed, AdaComm) under the Gaussian accountant, which does not charge for the information their schedules reveal, so their reported budgets were not valid DP guarantees; v4 refuses such strategies under DP, adds the data-independent TierFed-DP planner, and replaces the equal-$\sigma$ table by a matched-$\varepsilon$ comparison with Poisson sampling (Section~S3). Items (1)--(10) were found in an audit of the earlier manuscript, items (11)--(12) during the v3 runs and item (13) in the v4 revision; all affected runs were deleted and rerun. With these corrections the earlier central finding, that fixed multi-rate schedules lose about 0.05~AUROC to personalised baselines, does not reproduce and is retracted. Every item maps to a code change in the released repository (\texttt{ROADMAP.md}, section 0) and to a test.

\section{S2. Derivation of Proposition 1}
\label{supp:proof}
We follow the cooperative-SGD framework of Wang and Joshi [JMLR 2021, Thm.~1], which bounds the averaged gradient norm of local-update SGD in terms of the per-round deviation of client iterates from the virtual average. Write the shared parameter as $\theta=(\theta_g)_{g}$ with disjoint blocks. For site $k$ and block $g$ let $\theta^{(t)}_{k,g}$ be the iterate after $t$ local steps since block $g$ was last synchronised and $\bar\theta^{(t)}_g$ the (virtual) average across sites.

\begin{lemma}[Blockwise deviation]
Under A1--A3, for $\eta\le c_0/(LK_{\max}\tau_{\max})$ and any $t\le K_g\tau_{\max}$,
\[
\frac1N\sum_k\mathbb E\big\|\theta^{(t)}_{k,g}-\bar\theta^{(t)}_g\big\|^2\ \le\ c\,\eta^2 t^2\,(\Gamma_g+\sigma_g^2)\ \le\ c\,\eta^2K_g^2\tau_{\max}^2(\Gamma_g+\sigma_g^2).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Identical to Lemma 2 of Wang and Joshi restricted to the coordinates of block $g$: the deviation after $t$ steps is a sum of $t$ stochastic-gradient differences, each with second moment bounded by $\Gamma_g+\sigma_g^2$ by (A2)--(A3), and the smoothness term is absorbed by the step-size condition. The restriction to a block is valid because the gradient of block $g$ is bounded blockwise by the same assumptions.
\end{proof}

The descent-lemma step of the cited theorem bounds $\mathbb E\|\nabla F(\bar\theta_r)\|^2$ by the progress term, the variance term and $L^2$ times the \emph{total} deviation $\frac1N\sum_k\|\theta_k-\bar\theta\|^2$. Because the blocks are disjoint, the total deviation is the sum over $g$ of the blockwise deviations, each bounded by the lemma with its own $K_g$; summing and averaging over rounds gives Proposition~1 of the main text with constants inherited from the cited theorem. We make no claim about the constants beyond their existence. Setting $K_g=1$ for a single block returns the cited bound with $\tau$ local steps.

\textbf{What the controller sees.} By the same lemma the dispersion of the received slices of tier $g$ after $K_g$ rounds is of order $\eta^2K_g^2\tau^2(\Gamma_g+\sigma_g^2)$; normalising by $\sum_j\|\bar\theta_j\|^2$ gives the scale-free $\hat\Gamma_g$ of Eq.~(7) of the main text (the dispersion estimator). The controller therefore minimises $\sum_g K_g^2\hat\Gamma_g$ where $\hat\Gamma_g$ already carries the factor $K_g^2$ of the interval that produced it; it is a feedback loop on a proxy, and its stability is observed (Fig.~4 of the main text), not proved.

\section{S3. Differential-privacy accounting}
Per-client clipping to $C$ makes the $\ell_2$ sensitivity of the sum to the addition or removal of one client exactly $C$, and the fixed denominator $qN$ does not depend on the realised cohort. With Poisson sampling at rate $q$ each release is the subsampled Gaussian mechanism, whose RDP at integer order $\alpha$ is $\frac1{\alpha-1}\log\sum_{k=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{k}(1-q)^{\alpha-k}q^k\exp\frac{k^2-k}{2\sigma^2}$ [Mironov, Talwar and Zhang 2019, Sec.~3.3]; for $q=1$ it is $\alpha/(2\sigma^2)$ [Mironov 2017, Prop.~7]. Composition over the $R_{\mathrm{tx}}$ planned releases is additive, and $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP follows from [Mironov 2017, Prop.~3], minimised over $\alpha\in\{2,\dots,256\}$ (and a fine fractional grid for $q=1$). The accountant is valid only if the set of release rounds is independent of the private data, which is why only FedAvg, FedProx, FedPer, the fixed period and \tierfed{}-DP are run under DP in the main text. The sum is computed in log space; the implementation matches the TensorFlow-Privacy MNIST reference ($\varepsilon\approx3.0$ at $q=256/60000$, $\sigma=1.1$, 60 epochs) to within 0.05. Model selection on held-out data is not covered by the budget, so DP results are final-round values.

Table~\ref{tab:dp-legacy} and Fig.~\ref{fig:privacy-legacy} contain the clearest effect in this study. Under client-level DP, every transmitting round is a release that the accountant charges for and into which noise is injected, so the number of transmitting rounds sets both the budget and the cumulative noise. At $\sigma=1$, full-rate FedAvg collapses to \num{dpFedavgSigmaOneAuroc} and FedPer reaches \num{dpFedperSigmaOneAuroc}, both at $\varepsilon=\num{dpFedperSigmaOneEps}$; \tierfed{} reaches \num{dpTierfedSigmaOneAuroc} at $\varepsilon=\num{dpTierfedSigmaOneEps}$ (\tierfed{} minus FedPer \num{dpTfVsFedperSigmaOneDiff}, $p=\num{dpTfVsFedperSigmaOneP}$). At $\sigma=2$ the margin over FedPer is \num{dpTfVsFedperSigmaTwoDiff} ($p=\num{dpTfVsFedperSigmaTwoP}$) at less than half the budget. Against AdaComm, the strongest round-sparse baseline, the comparison is mixed and we report it as such: level at $\sigma=0.5$ (\num{dpTfVsAdacommSigmaHalfDiff}, $p=\num{dpTfVsAdacommSigmaHalfP}$) and $\sigma=1$ (\num{dpTfVsAdacommSigmaOneDiff}, $p=\num{dpTfVsAdacommSigmaOneP}$), better at $\sigma=2$ (\num{dpTfVsAdacommSigmaTwoDiff}, $p=\num{dpTfVsAdacommSigmaTwoP}$), and worse at $\sigma=4$ (\num{dpTfVsAdacommSigmaFourDiff}, $p=\num{dpTfVsAdacommSigmaFourP}$), where AdaComm's eight releases incur less noise than \tierfed{}'s eighteen. The operational reading is that, under DP, the number of rounds a site transmits is the dominant design variable, and a communication quota is also a privacy quota. None of the budgets reported is claimed to constitute meaningful privacy; they are reported as computed so that the trade-off is measured correctly.
