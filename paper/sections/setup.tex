\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Every v6 run uses the validation-selected protocol of Section~\ref{sec:system}. Seven experiment families are run, each with every listed method on the same seeds. They are the main comparison (13 configurations, 10 seeds), the iso-byte frontier (each method with its own knob swept; 5 seeds for the added settings), the heterogeneity sweep (Dirichlet $\alpha\in\{0.1,0.3,1,10\}$, 5 seeds), the ResNet backbone (6 seeds), matched-$\varepsilon$ DP at $q=1$ (6 seeds), the timing side channel (5 seeds per world) and peak-aware staggering. Table~\ref{tab:setup} lists the hyperparameters. Results of the earlier final-round protocol, on compression, real institutions, imaging, scaling and a wide model, are in the supplement with that protocol stated.

\textbf{Baselines and bounds.} \emph{Full rate:} FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017}, FedProx~\cite{li2020fedprox} and FedPer~\cite{arivazhagan2019}, the reference for the non-inferiority tests. \emph{Round-sparse:} AdaComm~\cite{wang2019adacomm}, LAG~\cite{chen2018lag} with sample weighting and, for comparison with earlier versions, with the equal weights used there, a FedLAMA-style per-layer interval rule reimplemented from its published description~\cite{lee2023fedlama}, and fixed periods on (\ref{eq:due}) with the same private head and warm-up as \tierfed{}. \emph{Bounds:} local training without communication, a centralised MLP trained on the pooled training splits of all sites for the same number of passes, and gradient boosting~\cite{ke2017lightgbm} as a reference for model quality; all three are scored on the same per-site test splits with the round, epoch or iteration chosen on validation. SCAFFOLD with Adam is a poor match for its control variates, and its SGD arm is in the supplement. Every method uses one architecture table, one evaluation protocol and one bit-accounting rule, and nothing is chosen on test data.

\textbf{Differential privacy.} FedPer, fixed periods $K=4$ and $K=8$, one-shot federation (no warm-up, one release in round $R$, after a data-independent broadcast of the initial model) and the planner run at $\varepsilon^\star=4$, $\delta=10^{-5}$, $q=1$, $C=1$, each with $z$ calibrated to its own release count. Local training is the $\varepsilon=0$ reference on the same splits. The $q<1$ sweeps over $\varepsilon^\star\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ on 20 and 100 sites are in the supplement as a sensitivity analysis.

\textbf{Timing side channel.} One target site holds half of its labels flipped, or none, and an observer who sees only which rounds carry traffic and how many bytes each site sends tries to tell the two worlds apart; an AUC of 0.5 means that the schedule reveals nothing, and the fixed period is the control.

\textbf{Statistics.} All comparisons are paired across seeds. Two primary contrasts were fixed in the code before the runs, with the metric and both thresholds: \tierfed{} against FedPer, and \tierfed{} against the best uniform period at matched bytes, chosen on validation among the uniform periods whose upload is at most 5\% above \tierfed{}'s; every other comparison is exploratory. Tests are two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests and intervals are percentile bootstrap intervals over seeds. The gate first required a Holm-corrected $p<0.05$, which 5 or 6 seeds cannot reach, so the second contrast is read from its interval: a setting counts as a gain only when the 95\% interval excludes zero and the mean gain is at least 0.005 AUROC\ifflag{auRouteA}{}{; no setting passes either rule}. A difference below 0.005 is not called relevant, whatever its $p$. Non-inferiority to FedPer is tested one-sided, $H_0:\mathbb E[d]\le-\Delta_{\mathrm{NI}}$ with $d=\mathrm{AUROC}_{\text{method}}-\mathrm{AUROC}_{\text{FedPer}}$, by the signed-rank test on $d+\Delta_{\mathrm{NI}}$ at $\Delta_{\mathrm{NI}}=0.01$ and, as a sensitivity margin, 0.005. Repeated runs are bit-identical on one machine. On a second machine every data-independent schedule \ifflag{xmachineBytesIdentical}{sends the same bytes}{changes its bytes}, but the AUROC of a single run can move by up to \num{xmachineAurocDiff} (Section~\ref{sec:results-check}), so every comparison is computed within one machine.
