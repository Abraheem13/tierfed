\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What the evidence supports.} First, synchronising less costs no relevant accuracy here: every federated method except AdaComm reaches FedPer's validation-selected AUROC within \num{auFedSpread}, the multi-rate periods at about a third of the upload, and the earlier lead of sparse schedules came from reading the final round. Second, whether per-tier intervals can beat a uniform period can be bounded before training: $\Psi$ is \num{auGateSpecialtyPsi}\% on the MLP, where \tierfed{} reduces to the uniform period, and \num{auGateResnetPsi}\% on the ResNet, where \ifflag{auGateResnetWins}{the per-tier split beats the best uniform period at matched bytes by \num{auGateResnetGain} AUROC [\num{auGateResnetLo}, \num{auGateResnetHi}]}{the per-tier split chosen by the closed form still lands \num{auGateResnetGain} AUROC [\num{auGateResnetLo}, \num{auGateResnetHi}] from the best uniform period at matched bytes; $\Psi$ bounds a surrogate, and a large bound did not become accuracy}. Third, phases cap the payload of any single round at an unchanged mean, which a uniform period cannot do, at the price of more radio wake-ups. Fourth, under client-level DP the schedule is part of the mechanism\ifflag{auTimingLagLeaks}{: a schedule that adapts to client data shows in the traffic,}{,} and at 20 sites every releasing schedule is far below local training.

\textbf{What it does not support.} \ifflag{auRouteA}{\tierfed{} is not more accurate than a well-chosen uniform period where $\Psi$ is small, which includes the main benchmark and every label-skew setting tested.}{\tierfed{} is not more accurate than a well-chosen uniform period in any setting tested.} Its quota alone is no advantage, because a uniform period meets a quota $\rho$ with $K=\lceil1/\rho\rceil$, and the privacy planner cannot differentiate tiers under its flat prior.

\textbf{Limits of the evidence.} The sites of the main benchmark are specialty buckets, not hospitals, and the four real institutions have a few hundred patients each. The link model is a cost translation of logged bits, not a network measurement (Section~\ref{sec:netmodel}). Proposition~\ref{prop:bound} assumes block-separable smoothness and equal local steps and is for SGD, while the benchmark uses Adam and unequal steps; Proposition~\ref{prop:cuberoot} is exact for the programme, not for accuracy, and the stability of the dispersion feedback is observed, not proved. The privacy unit is the site: under record-level DP-SGD~\cite{liu2022crosssilo,liu2024recordlevel} every local step is charged, so the synchronisation schedule does not change $\varepsilon$ under standard composition. The attacks are generic; TabLeak~\cite{vero2023tableak} and LiRA~\cite{carlini2022lira} are not run. The main table and the DP comparison use 10 and 6 seeds and the other families 5 or 6; the FT-Transformer, DermaMNIST, record-level DP and site dropouts were not run under the new protocol, and on a second machine the AUROC of a single run moved by up to \num{xmachineAurocDiff}.

\textbf{Practical guidance.} Select the reported round on validation. Measure $\Psi$ at the end of warm-up and, when it is small, use a uniform period $K=\lceil1/\rho\rceil$, staggering the tiers if the per-round peak matters. Under client-level DP, fix the schedule before training, calibrate $z$ to its release count and compare with local training before federating.
