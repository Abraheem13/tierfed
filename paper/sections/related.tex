\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Aggregation under heterogeneity.} FedAvg~\cite{mcmahan2017} averages all parameters every round. FedProx~\cite{li2020fedprox} adds a proximal term; SCAFFOLD~\cite{karimireddy2020} corrects client drift with control variates at twice the payload. Surveys catalogue many variants~\cite{kairouz2021,li2022niid}; all synchronise everything every round.

\textbf{Personalisation.} FedPer~\cite{arivazhagan2019} keeps a private classification head; FedRep~\cite{collins2021} alternates representation and head updates; LG-FedAvg~\cite{liang2020} keeps lower layers local and shares the top; FedBABU~\cite{oh2022} trains only the body during federation; Pillutla \emph{et al.}~\cite{pillutla2022} give a general treatment of partial-model personalisation; Ditto~\cite{li2021ditto} and FedBN~\cite{li2021fedbn} personalise through regularisation and normalisation statistics. FedPer is the strongest baseline in our experiments and is the reference for every non-inferiority test. None of these methods controls \emph{when} the shared part is sent.

\textbf{Communication frequency.} Local SGD with a fixed period is analysed in~\cite{stich2019,wang2021coop}. AdaComm~\cite{wang2019adacomm} adapts a single period from the training loss to minimise an error-versus-runtime bound; LAG~\cite{chen2018lag} lets a client skip an upload when its update has changed little since the last one; FedPAQ~\cite{reisizadeh2020} combines periodic averaging with quantisation. These are the closest prior work to a measurement-driven schedule, and we implement AdaComm and LAG as baselines rather than discussing them away. They differ from \tierfed{} in three respects: they use one period for the whole model (AdaComm) or a per-client rather than per-block decision (LAG), they do not accept an explicit budget, and they do not withhold a personalised head.

\textbf{Layer-wise aggregation.} FedLAMA~\cite{lee2023fedlama} assigns per-layer intervals from measured discrepancy; pFedLA~\cite{ma2022pfedla} learns per-layer weights with a hypernetwork; L-DAWA~\cite{rehman2023} weights layers by divergence in self-supervised FL; FedCDA~\cite{wang2024fedcda} aggregates across rounds. \tierfed{} differs in casting the schedule as a budget-constrained minimisation of the bound term $\sum_g K_g^2\Gamma_g$, in exposing $\rho$ as the single operator-facing knob, and in excluding the head from aggregation. We report a reimplementation of FedLAMA from its published description and label it as such.

\textbf{Compression.} Quantisation, sparsification and sketching reduce message size~\cite{sattler2020,rothchild2020}. They are orthogonal to message timing; Section~\ref{sec:results-comp} shows the two compose multiplicatively.

\textbf{FL over wireless and 6G networks.} The communication cost of FL over wireless links has been studied through joint learning-and-communication design~\cite{chen2021joint}, over-the-air computation~\cite{yang2020ota}, and surveys of FL in mobile edge networks~\cite{lim2020,niknam2020}; 6G roadmaps place distributed learning among native network services~\cite{letaief2019,saad2020}. Our contribution is complementary: we do not design the physical layer, we give the application a knob that maps directly onto a slice or window quota and we cost every method on published link profiles.

\textbf{Privacy and attack surface.} Client-level DP for FL follows McMahan \emph{et al.}~\cite{mcmahan2018dp} with R\'enyi accounting~\cite{mironov2017}. Gradient and update inversion~\cite{zhu2019dlg,geiping2020} and membership inference~\cite{yeom2018} measure what an adversary recovers. We evaluate all three on every method, on the slices each method actually transmits.
